  Floor and water both stay exact on unreleased rings; water retention reaches
  a monotone closure when a retained neighbour extends such a ring.
- An input-only source needs zero actual buffer and flow at every recorded
  sample, not merely zero output. In footprints containing water above target,
  freeze every below-target floor, keeping dry capacity exactly invariant for
  every water state. In the others, check the paired proposed startup floor
  and water: availability must remain nonpositive. Raises and film removal
  must ship together; either alone can activate an intake.
- Sources with actual withdrawal or output, networks, water bodies and measured
  hydraulic-floor offsets keep both floor and startup water unchanged.

The intake identity is `availability = sum(water) - sum(max(target-floor,0))`.
Thus a floor edit shifts availability by a state-independent capacity change.
Six sampled nonpositive sums alone are not proof of inactivity: an active
intake at equilibrium can have the same sums.
