# Is the candidate's roof form right?

Each sheet shows one real building. The three left panels are references:

1. a spring-2025 aerial photo, north up, with the building's outline in yellow (only the outlined building is the subject);
2. a lidar-measured surface seen from above, north up (pale = built or bare surfaces, green = vegetation, which can hide a roof);
3. the same lidar surface seen obliquely from the south-east.

The two right panels are one 3D model (the CANDIDATE) of that building, from above (north up) and obliquely from the south-east.

For each sheet:

1. From the references alone, name the real building's dominant roof form: `flat`, `flat-multilevel` (several flat levels), `shed`, `gable`, `hip`, `cross-gable` (gables meeting at angles, or a main gable with gabled wings), `complex` (several forms; say which dominates), or `unobservable` (references do not show the roof well enough).
2. From the references alone, give the dominant ridge direction as a compass axis: `N-S`, `NE-SW`, `E-W`, `NW-SE`, or `none` (flat, pyramidal, or unobservable).
3. Name the candidate's dominant roof form and ridge direction the same way.
4. Verdict on the candidate: `correct` (dominant form matches and ridge axis within about 30 degrees, or both flat), `partly` (right family but a clearly visible major part is wrong, missing or spurious), `wrong`, or `cannot-judge` (reference unobservable).
5. A note of at most 20 words, naming the main error if any.

Judge the dominant form, not small details: dormers, chimneys and roof-top equipment do not matter.
